Blocking launch

Four things must be true before a single ad runs 1. Three public claims are false and are live right now. 2. There is no legal paperwork to take money with. 3. The AI endpoint has no authentication or rate limiting, so AI spend is uncapped. 4. There is nothing to buy.

None of those are hard. Three of the four are a week's work. The fourth is the pricing decision in the next section.

2Pricing - the decision

You said you're not sure what to charge. Here's one recommendation with the reasoning, not a menu.

Recommendation £24.99 per year. Annual only at launch. A monthly option at £4.99 exists purely to make the annual look obvious - price it so nobody picks it.

Why annual first

DIY is episodic. You fix a tap, then nothing happens for four months. A monthly subscription on an app people open a handful of times a year churns hard - you'd spend all your effort re-acquiring people who already paid you once. One annual decision, no cancellation problem, and the money lands up front where it can pay for the campaign.

Why £24.99 and not £4.99 a month

Because the honest comparison is enormous. A plumber or electrician charges £80-150 just to turn up in most of the UK. One avoided call-out pays for three to six years of DIY DAY. That is the marketing line, and it's true, which is why it beats a fake discount.

The conversion moment

Don't ask for money on arrival. Ask at the end of diagnosis three - they've just watched it work three times, they've got a shopping list in front of them, and the next thing they'll do is hit the wall. That's the only moment where £25 feels cheap. Everything else is a guess.

Build constraint - read before any paywall work The three free diagnoses must be counted on the server, not in the browser. Right now the endpoint has no auth, so a browser-side counter can be bypassed trivially, and every bypass costs you real AI credits. Auth first, then the meter, then the paywall. That order, or it leaks.

5The fix list

Every item here is in a file I've read. Owner tells you who does it.

Take the three false claims out

Claim 1 - the 25% Where: src/routes/parts.tsx (the promo block, around line 230) and src/routes/index.tsx (Membership section, around line 157).
Says: "Up to 25% off when you shop through DIY DAY" and "unlock trade-price deals across every stockist".
Reality: there is no discount scheme, no trade account, no code. The parts page builds a public search URL and sends them to the shop.
Replace with: the call-out maths. "A plumber charges £90 to turn up. DIY DAY Pro is £24.99 a year." True, bigger, and it doesn't need a scheme behind it.
Claim 2 - live prices Where: src/routes/index.tsx, stockists section: "Live prices, deep links - no fake numbers, no redirect tricks."
Reality: src/lib/pricing.ts is a hand-written keyword table - taps £18-120, boilers £600-1800. Its own comment says "never live retail prices".
Note: the app itself is honest, it labels every figure "Est." It's the landing page that lies about it. So fix the page, keep the app.
Claim 3 - no sign-up needed Where: src/routes/index.tsx says "Free · No sign-up needed · All major UK stockists". src/routes/parts.tsx shows a padlock and "Sign up to unlock member prices" to anyone logged out.
Fix: decide which is true. Recommendation - parts search is free (it costs you nothing and it's your best affiliate surface), and the paywall sits on diagnoses, not parts. That kills the contradiction and gives the free tier more to be generous with.

6The legal floor

You cannot take a card payment without this. It's the least fun section and the one that costs the most if skipped.

On the record I'm not a solicitor and this isn't legal advice. What follows is what a UK consumer app needs to have before it charges money - the drafting should come from someone qualified, or a reputable template reviewed by one.

The safety gate - do this properly

The one thing that should not be left to a prompt Right now the only guardrail is an instruction inside the AI prompt asking it to open with a red warning on gas and mains electricity. A prompt is a hope, not a rule. It needs to be enforced in code: detect gas, boiler, consumer unit, fuse board and mains terms before the AI call, and return a hard stop that says get a Gas Safe registered engineer or a qualified electrician, with no repair steps attached. Paid or free, no exceptions.

This is the single highest-risk item on the whole page. A consumer app that walks someone through a boiler because the model didn't feel like warning that day is a different order of problem to a wrong price on a landing page.

7The campaign - three lanes

You're planning to run this alongside InstructSite and AgentTrac. There's a trap in that: the three audiences are not the same person, so "we'll all promote each other" does nothing. Each lane needs a different mechanic.

Lane 1

DIY DAY → homeowners

The buyer. Episodic, searches when something breaks, price-sensitive but terrified of call-out fees.

Mechanic 14 tips become 14 short videos. One hook: "a plumber charges £90 to turn up, this is £24.99 a year." Publish the tips free - they're the proof the app knows what it's doing.
Lane 2

InstructSite → builders → their clients

This is the strong one. InstructSite's users are contractors; their clients are exactly the homeowners DIY DAY serves. It's not an ad swap, it's distribution.

Mechanic Add a DIY DAY card to the handover pack. Every InstructSite job that completes hands the homeowner a "here's the app for the jobs after we've gone" page. Builders look helpful, you get a warm lead at the exact moment someone's about to start doing their own snagging.
Lane 3

AgentTrac → owners & operators

Weakest direct fit - business owners, not necessarily mid-repair. But the strongest story.

Mechanic Don't sell DIY to them. Sell the build: "one bloke, one agent, a live consumer app in a month" is the AgentTrac case study. DIY DAY becomes the proof, not the product, in that lane.
The sequencing that matters Lane 2 first. It's the only lane where you're reaching a homeowner at the precise moment they need you, through a channel you already own, at zero cost per acquisition. Lanes 1 and 3 both need spend or effort to find strangers. Do the free one first, learn what converts, then buy attention.

What the campaign can honestly say

  • "Three free repairs, no card, no sign-up." True once you ship the free tier.
  • "A plumber charges £90 to turn up. This is £24.99 a year." True and checkable.
  • "Written by a site manager with 20-odd years on the tools." True, and the strongest asset you have. Use your name and your job - it's why the tips read like a builder wrote them.
  • What it must not say: 25% off, live prices, or "no sign-up" anywhere near the parts page.
